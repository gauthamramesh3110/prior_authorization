## 1. Product applicability, population, and benefit scope

This policy establishes medical-necessity criteria for bone mineral density (BMD) measurement requested under Harbor Choice PPO for members age 18 years and older. It applies to outpatient screening, evaluation of suspected or established skeletal fragility, assessment of defined causes of bone loss, and serial measurement that informs a documented management decision.

The requested service must be a bone-density measurement. The authorization service identifier is SNOMED-CT **312681000, Bone density scan (procedure)**. Because that identifier does not specify the technique or skeletal site, the order must identify both. A nuclear medicine bone scan, diagnostic CT performed for another purpose, or a body-composition study does not become a BMD examination through use of this identifier.

This policy applies only to the named product. Pediatric requests and requests under a different commercial, Medicare, or Medicaid product require the policy and benefit provisions applicable to that product. Being outside this document's population is an applicability finding, not a finding that the service is medically unnecessary.

Member eligibility, service coverage, provider participation, and authorization requirements are verified separately for the requested service date. A medical-necessity finding does not independently establish those benefits. Preventive classification and member cost sharing follow the applicable benefit contract; this policy does not determine them from age or a diagnosis code alone.

This document is the current policy for the named product, effective October 9, 2026. Apply its clinical provisions to requests for services on or after that date, subject to the separate benefit checks above.

## 2. Medical-necessity decision structure and qualifying routes

A standard BMD request meets this policy when **all** of the following are established:

1. The product, adult population, and requested measurement are within section 1.
2. **At least one** indication route in sections 4 through 14 is supported. The routes are alternatives; they are not a combined checklist.
3. The proposed method and sites meet sections 3 and 19, as applicable.
4. An initial or repeat-study purpose is established, and a repeat request meets section 15 or an approved exception in section 16.
5. The evidence required for the selected route is sufficient under sections 20 and 21.

A documented exception under section 23 may establish medical necessity when a standard clinical route is not met. It requires an individual physician determination and does not change product eligibility or establish coverage for a service excluded by the benefit contract.

**Osteoporosis and an osteoporosis-related fracture are separate qualifying routes. Neither is required for an eligible screening or secondary-bone-loss request.** A member does not have to try osteoporosis medication before a clinically indicated baseline measurement. No scan result is required to justify the initial scan that would produce it.

For each applicable requirement, record one of: **supported**, **not supported by the supplied facts**, **insufficient evidence**, or **not applicable**. Missing information is not proof that a criterion failed. Do not assess unselected routes as failed requirements.

The medical reviewer evaluates clinical necessity. Administrative routing, availability of an automated pathway, and completeness of the clinical assessment are separate findings. A referral for physician review is a process step, not a clinical criterion that a patient can meet.

## 3. Covered DXA methods and selection of skeletal sites

For a standard assessment, request dual-energy X-ray absorptiometry (DXA) of the lumbar spine and hip. One examination may include the customary measurements at both sites; they do not represent two independent serial studies. Specify any site that cannot be acquired or interpreted and the reason. [R2]

Forearm DXA at the **33% radius** is eligible when a central site is unusable, the member exceeds the central scanner's weight limit, or hyperparathyroidism requires assessment at that site. It may supplement usable central measurements when that additional site answers a distinct clinical question. Routine addition of a peripheral study to an otherwise adequate central study is not supported. [R2]

Document site limitations, such as hardware, bilateral hip replacement, positioning limitations, or relevant artifact. A contraindication affecting one site does not automatically invalidate all other sites. The interpreting clinician determines which measurements remain technically usable.

Apply the same indication and frequency requirements to a forearm study. Selection of another site does not restart the repeat-study clock or create a second screening entitlement.

A request for a method other than DXA requires the individual review described in section 19. General statements such as "bone scan" or "osteoporosis test" are insufficient to select the measurement method; request a precise order when that ambiguity affects authorization.

## 4. Age-based initial screening

Initial screening DXA is eligible through either of these alternative routes:

| Route | Required population fact | Required request purpose |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 4.A | Woman age **65 years or older** on the requested date | Initial screening for osteoporosis |
| 4.B | Man age **70 years or older** on the requested date | Initial screening for osteoporosis |

An additional fracture, osteoporosis diagnosis, risk-score threshold, or prior medication trial is not required for either age-based route. Confirm whether a prior usable study exists. If it does, evaluate the request as a repeat study under section 15 rather than treating a new provider's first order as the member's first scan.

The women's screening route reflects the USPSTF recommendation. The men's route reflects specialty guidance; the USPSTF's insufficient-evidence statement for men is not a recommendation against testing. [R1, R3]

Use verified age at the requested service date. When recorded demographic information does not describe the member's relevant menopausal, gonadal, or hormone-exposure context, obtain the clinician's applicable context rather than assigning a route from a name or gender marker. Defined medical-risk routes and section 23 remain available regardless of age-based screening eligibility.

## 5. Screening in postmenopausal women younger than 65

This route applies to postmenopausal women age **40 through 64 years** without an already established diagnostic indication. Require all of the following:

1. Documented postmenopausal status.
2. At least one risk factor beyond menopausal status: low body weight, parental hip fracture, current smoking, or excess alcohol exposure.
3. A recorded clinical risk assessment supporting screening now. [R1]

Accept one of these assessment approaches:

- **Osteoporosis Self-Assessment Tool (OST): score below 2.** Record age, weight in kilograms, calculation date, and result.
- **Osteoporosis Risk Assessment Instrument (ORAI): score 9 or higher.** Record the result and the age, weight, and estrogen-use inputs.
- **FRAX or another validated assessment:** record the tool, relevant inputs, result, and the clinician's explanation of why the estimated risk warrants BMD measurement. This route does not impose a single FRAX percentage as a screening cutoff. [R1]

For this screening route, low body weight means BMI below 20 kg/m2 or weight below 60 kg; these are plan-selected risk-factor triggers. For excess alcohol, record the amount, frequency, and unit used in the clinician's assessment or tool input. The documented assessment must connect the risk findings to the decision to screen. A positive OST or ORAI result satisfies the risk-assessment component; an additional FRAX calculation is not required. A risk assessment can also identify an applicable diagnostic or secondary-cause route that does not depend on this screening pathway.

If the result is below a tool's screening threshold, document a concrete reason why the tool underestimates this member's risk and refer the exception under section 23. Do not substitute a treatment threshold for the screening threshold. A woman younger than 40 with premature ovarian insufficiency or another defined medical cause is assessed under section 8, not excluded solely because this screening route begins at 40.

## 6. Risk-based assessment in men age 50 through 69

A man age **50 through 69 years** is eligible for initial DXA when at least one of the following is documented and the order states that BMD will guide fracture-risk or osteoporosis management:

- Prior adult fracture with a clinical concern for skeletal fragility.
- Low body weight.
- Current cigarette smoking.
- A disease, hormonal deficiency, or medication exposure covered by sections 8 through 11. [R3]

For this route, define low body weight as **BMI below 20 kg/m2** or **weight below 60 kg**. These are Harbor Choice PPO screening triggers, not diagnostic definitions of osteoporosis. Record the measurement date and the available height and weight; do not infer low body weight from appearance or an undocumented description.

One qualifying factor is sufficient; multiple factors and a positive risk calculator are not additionally required. If the member has established osteoporosis or a fragility fracture, use section 7. Men younger than 50 may qualify through the diagnostic, secondary-cause, medication, or exception routes; age alone does not exclude those routes.

If a fracture was clearly high energy and is the only submitted factor, require the clinician's explanation of the concern for underlying skeletal fragility. The injury is not automatically an osteoporosis-related fracture.

## 7. Established osteoporosis, low-trauma fracture, and suspicious prior imaging

Initial or clinically indicated follow-up BMD is eligible when any one of the following is documented:

**7.A Established osteoporosis:** a treating clinician identifies osteoporosis and requests a baseline measurement, reassessment, or monitoring. Accept a prior valid densitometry report or a documented clinical diagnosis with its basis. The diagnosis does not have to be accompanied by a fracture. Apply section 15 only when the request is for a repeat study; an initial baseline has no prior-study interval requirement.

**7.B Fragility fracture:** the clinician documents a fracture, its site and date or approximate period, and a low-trauma mechanism or other clinical basis for classifying it as fragility related. A fall from standing height or less is a typical low-trauma mechanism. [R1] Explain how BMD contributes to the subsequent care plan; the fracture does not have to remain unhealed or be coded as an active injury.

**7.C Suspicious prior imaging:** an existing imaging report or an adequately sourced clinician account describes low bone mass, vertebral compression, or another finding for which DXA would resolve a stated osteoporosis-assessment question. An unqualified description of back pain alone does not establish this route.

A historical osteoporosis-related fracture can remain relevant even when its condition record has an end date. Evaluate the clinical history rather than requiring an active fracture episode. Historical relevance does not remove repeat-study requirements.

A fracture attributed solely to malignancy, infection, or a major traumatic event does not establish 7.B without a separate documented concern for skeletal fragility. Review another applicable route before concluding that the request is unsupported.

DXA must not be used as a prerequisite that delays indicated fracture care or secondary fracture prevention. It does not replace imaging needed to diagnose an acute fracture. [R4]

## 8. Defined diseases and conditions associated with bone loss

Initial DXA is eligible when the clinician documents one of the following conditions **and** identifies a BMD-dependent assessment or management decision:

| Clinical category | Required clinical context |
| --- | --- |
| Primary hyperparathyroidism | Established disease and planned assessment of skeletal involvement or management |
| Hyperthyroidism | Ongoing thyrotoxicosis or a documented history of prolonged uncontrolled disease relevant to current bone risk |
| Gonadal or ovarian dysfunction | Sustained hypogonadism, premature ovarian insufficiency, surgical loss of gonadal function without adequate replacement, or prolonged amenorrhea with documented hormone deficiency |
| Malabsorption or nutritional disease | Clinically significant celiac disease, inflammatory bowel disease with malabsorption, chronic malnutrition, or bariatric surgery with ongoing skeletal risk |
| Rheumatoid arthritis or severe systemic inflammatory disease | Documented disease and an individualized fracture-risk or bone-health assessment question |
| Prolonged immobilization | Substantial loss of weight-bearing activity lasting or expected to last at least **3 months**, with concern for disuse bone loss |

These categories are informed by recognized secondary causes and risk factors. [R7] The three-month immobilization threshold is a plan-selected trigger; it is not a universal diagnostic threshold.

A disease name on a problem list is insufficient when neither its relevant activity nor the reason for measurement is described. Conversely, do not request a new laboratory workup merely because the clinical account already establishes the applicable condition. Only request laboratory or specialist information that resolves a specific uncertainty.

Age-based screening thresholds do not apply to these diagnostic routes. A member younger than 50 can qualify without a positive FRAX result or a prior T-score of -2.5.

Other medical causes, including complex endocrine disease or a specialist-supported condition not listed here, require section 23. Chronic kidney disease has its own route in section 9. Requests for specialized spinal-cord-injury sites require the site-specific review in section 19.

## 9. Chronic kidney disease and transplant-associated skeletal risk

DXA may be medically necessary in **CKD stages G3a through G5D** when there is evidence of CKD-related mineral and bone disorder or another osteoporosis risk factor **and** the result will influence a documented treatment decision. A transplant recipient with osteoporosis risk factors may also qualify when the result would alter management. [R8]

Require the current renal stage or transplant context, the relevant skeletal-risk finding, and the specific decision the treating clinician plans to inform. Do not require every mineral-metabolism laboratory result for every request. Where laboratory abnormalities or suspected renal osteodystrophy materially affect the interpretation or planned treatment, obtain the clinician's explanation or relevant results.

CKD alone, without a skeletal-risk or management question, does not establish this route. BMD does not by itself characterize the type of renal bone disorder. [R8] Do not infer a diagnosis of postmenopausal osteoporosis solely from a low BMD value in a complex renal case.

An initial CKD-related assessment does not require failure of osteoporosis medication. Repeat studies follow section 15. An interval exception or uncertainty about the usefulness of measurement in the stated renal context requires physician review with the reason recorded.

## 10. Systemic glucocorticoid exposure

Initial DXA is eligible for an adult initiating or continuing **systemic glucocorticoids at a prednisone-equivalent dose of at least 2.5 mg/day for an anticipated or completed period longer than 3 months**. The planned course can establish eligibility before three months have elapsed; do not require the member to accumulate exposure before obtaining the baseline assessment. [R5]

Require the drug, route, daily dose or exposure schedule, start date, anticipated duration, and the stated bone-health assessment purpose. When another systemic agent is used, accept a clinician- or pharmacist-documented prednisone equivalence. Do not invent the conversion from an incomplete medication name or dose.

Topical, inhaled, or isolated intra-articular therapy does not satisfy this systemic daily-dose route by itself. The member may nevertheless qualify through another documented risk route or an exception. Intermittent high-dose courses, pulse treatment, and cumulative exposure that cannot be represented by the standard daily-dose rule require physician review of the actual exposure and clinical risk.

Long-term exposure can support repeat measurement at 12- to 24-month intervals when the result informs management; the detailed timing rules remain in section 15. [R5] The medication route does not automatically establish a need to repeat an adequate recent study immediately.

FRAX is not required for a member younger than 40 under this route. A risk calculator must not replace the documented dose and duration or be used to exclude a supported glucocorticoid assessment. [R5]

## 11. Other medications and hormone-suppressing treatment

Initial DXA is eligible for either of these alternative planned or ongoing treatment exposures:

- **Aromatase-inhibitor therapy** associated with estrogen deprivation.
- **Androgen-deprivation therapy** for which the treating clinician documents a skeletal-risk assessment plan. [R6, R7]

Submit the medication or regimen, expected duration, current or planned start date, and intended use of the result. A baseline assessment before treatment is eligible; a pre-existing osteoporosis diagnosis is not required.

Long-term anticonvulsant exposure may establish an assessment route when the record identifies the agent, planned or actual treatment longer than **3 months**, and the clinician's concern for medication-related bone loss. This exposure threshold is a plan-selected coverage trigger informed by payer practice; it does not assert that every anticonvulsant has the same skeletal effect. [R6]

Other medication-associated requests require a documented exposure and a physician-supported explanation under section 23. A medication class with an uncertain association is insufficient as a stand-alone checkbox. Routine calcium or vitamin D supplementation and physiologic thyroid replacement do not themselves establish a drug-related testing indication.

For a repeat study, provide the relevant prior measurement and what has changed or which ongoing management decision requires reassessment. A new prescription or renewal does not, by itself, justify resetting the interval.

## 12. Initial baseline and confirmation of an earlier non-DXA result

An initial baseline is eligible when one indication route in sections 4 through 11 is supported and no prior usable BMD measurement addresses the current clinical purpose. The clinician must identify the reason for testing; a prior abnormal T-score is not required.

A prior heel-ultrasound or other peripheral assessment does not automatically substitute for a diagnostic central DXA baseline. When central DXA is required to establish a usable measurement for the planned care, document the earlier method and the reason for confirmation. Such a request is not a routine repeat of a comparable DXA study. No routine 12- or 24-month waiting period is imposed solely because the earlier noncomparable measurement was recent. Confirm that it cannot answer the documented central-baseline question. [R6]

An absent copy of a previous report is different from an unusable examination. Before approving a duplicate solely because the report is unavailable, determine whether its date, method, and relevant results can be obtained or reliably described. Section 20 permits a sufficiently detailed, attributable clinician account when the original report cannot be obtained.

If a recent adequate central DXA already addresses the question, a provider change, a new referral, or a preference for another scanner does not establish another baseline. When a genuine technical limitation or unavoidable equipment change requires a new baseline, apply section 17 and, if the interval is under 12 months, section 16.

## 13. Monitoring osteoporosis treatment and planned treatment interruption

Serial DXA is eligible when the member is receiving osteoporosis pharmacotherapy, or is being assessed before or during a planned interruption of bisphosphonate therapy, and the result will inform a documented continuation, change, resumption, or monitoring decision. This section does not prescribe a medication or authorize its interruption.

Require the medication or relevant treatment history, initiation or material change date, the last usable study, and the intended decision. A generic statement of "follow-up" is insufficient if it does not identify what is being reassessed.

A first response assessment is ordinarily considered **12 to 24 months after therapy initiation or change**, with subsequent intervals tailored to the clinical course. [R4] Section 15 sets the plan's repeat-study requirements. An overdue assessment is not denied solely because it occurs later than 24 months.

Calcium or vitamin D alone does not establish the pharmacotherapy-monitoring route. A member using supplements alone may qualify through untreated low bone mass, age-based rescreening, a medical-risk route, or section 23.

Stable disease can justify a longer interval; continued therapy does not create an annual entitlement. When requesting annual monitoring because of ongoing high risk, identify the actual risk finding or treatment decision. Specialty guidance supports individual monitoring intervals rather than one interval for every treated member. [R9]

Do not require a new fracture, worsening T-score, or failure of two medications before accepting a supported monitoring request. An improved result does not retrospectively erase an established diagnosis or make clinically justified monitoring unnecessary. [R4]

## 14. Untreated low bone mass and repeat screening

Repeat DXA is eligible for an untreated member when there is prior low bone mass or an ongoing screening indication, and the clinician identifies a reassessment that could alter management. Use section 15 for timing.

For a request at the standard interval, accept a documented planned reassessment of fracture risk or a treatment decision. A new fracture or demonstrable worsening is not mandatory. For a request before the standard interval, identify a changed risk factor, expected rapid bone loss, or another documented exception.

Normal BMD does not create a requirement for routine annual testing. Lower-risk members with stable findings may reasonably have a longer interval than members approaching a treatment decision. [R4] This policy establishes eligibility boundaries, not a mandatory testing schedule.

A repeat request must identify the previous study date or explain why it is uncertain. Claims history can establish that a procedure occurred but does not establish its result, scan quality, or interpretation. Obtain the narrow information needed for the timing decision when those details matter.

FRAX can contribute to the clinical assessment but is not a substitute for valid comparison of BMD measurements. Do not require a pretreatment FRAX percentage from an already treated member as a condition of this route.

## 15. Standard repeat-study intervals and timing boundaries

For repeat DXA, **both** a continuing clinical indication and an eligible interval are required. The default interval is **at least 24 calendar months** between the last technically usable comparable BMD acquisition and the requested acquisition. A two-year default is consistent with common payer practice; it is a plan rule, not a universal clinical prohibition against earlier testing. [R6]

| Clinical purpose | Standard timing permitted by this policy | Additional timing evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Routine rescreening or untreated low bone mass without a material new risk | At least **24 months** after the last usable comparable study | Prior date and planned fracture-risk or treatment reassessment |
| First assessment after starting or materially changing osteoporosis medication | Ordinarily **12 to 24 months** after initiation/change, and at least **12 months** after the last usable comparable study | Treatment date and the response question; later testing remains eligible |
| Stable treated osteoporosis after the initial response assessment | At least **24 months** after the last usable comparable study | Continuing treatment and the planned management reassessment |
| Treated disease with an ongoing high-risk or rapid-change circumstance | At least **12 months** after the last usable comparable study | The risk circumstance and the decision that needs earlier reassessment |
| Continuing systemic steroid, aromatase-inhibitor, or androgen-deprivation exposure | At least **12 months** after the last usable comparable study | Ongoing exposure and intended management use |
| Planned bisphosphonate interruption or reassessment during interruption | At least **24 months**, or at least **12 months** with a documented new/high-risk circumstance | Treatment history and the continuation or resumption question |

For the 12-month high-risk route, document at least one of the following:

- A fragility fracture occurring after the last usable study.
- **Two or more** documented fragility fractures with an ongoing treatment-response question.
- A valid diagnostic-site **T-score of -3.5 or lower**, in a population for which T-score classification applies, with an active treatment-response question. This definition of very low BMD draws on specialty risk stratification. [R12]
- Continuing medication exposure supported by section 10 or 11.
- An active condition from section 8 or 9 with a clinician-documented reason to expect rapid bone loss before the 24-month interval.

Explain why reassessment at this interval will inform care. A bare "high risk" label is insufficient. In a younger population using Z-scores, do not apply the T-score trigger; use a supported exposure or secondary-cause route, or section 23.

A request **under 12 months** requires section 16. A technically invalid examination or confirmation after a different measurement method follows sections 12 and 17; do not treat an unusable scan as a valid monitoring baseline.

Measure intervals using acquisition dates and calendar-month anniversaries. If the corresponding day does not exist, use the last day of that month. The exact 12- or 24-month anniversary satisfies the respective minimum. Use these calendar-date boundaries rather than a counting convention from another product's billing provisions. A later acquisition is not ineligible because an expected follow-up window has passed.

## 16. Repeat DXA sooner than 12 months

An interval shorter than 12 months is eligible only after physician review establishes **all** of the following:

1. A supported indication or section 23 exception remains present.
2. A specific event or technical circumstance requires assessment now.
3. Waiting until the ordinary interval would materially affect a named management decision.
4. The proposed measurement can answer that question at this interval; when the purpose is detecting change, explain the expected change and the ability to distinguish it from measurement variability.
5. An adequate recent study or retrieval/reinterpretation of an existing study cannot reasonably answer the question.

Acceptable circumstances for consideration are a newly documented fragility fracture with a changed management question, a major new bone-depleting exposure or rapidly progressive medical cause, replacement of a technically invalid study, or an unavoidable new baseline following an equipment change. None produces automatic approval without the five findings above.

For a new fracture, explain the added value of another BMD measurement; fracture treatment or a clinically indicated therapy change must not wait for the scan merely to satisfy this policy.

For a trend assessment, provide the previous comparable measurement and relevant facility precision information when needed to judge whether an early difference can be interpreted. A technical replacement does not require a demonstrated density decline or a prior least-significant-change calculation. Measure replacement timing from the failed acquisition being replaced. Confirmation after a noncomparable peripheral method is governed by section 12 and is not forced into the comparable-DXA interval rule.

Record the physician's rationale, the circumstance supporting earlier testing, the authorized method and sites, and the service date. Approval applies to that request; it does not establish an ongoing interval shorter than 12 months.

## 17. Technical replacement, unavailable prior reports, and scanner changes

An examination may require replacement when it was not acquired successfully or the interpreting clinician identifies artifact, positioning error, equipment failure, or inadequate valid sites that prevent answering the clinical question. Submit the date, documented defect, and corrective plan. Repeating the same inadequate acquisition without correcting the identified problem is unsupported.

A replacement request follows the underlying indication route. It must identify why an existing valid site, reanalysis, or another appropriate site cannot answer the question. A request under 12 months also requires section 16; a confirmed technical replacement is assessed as replacement, not evidence of disease progression.

Prefer comparable follow-up measurements. If scanners are not cross-calibrated, numerical change between them cannot be treated as a measured treatment response. [R2] When an unavoidable equipment or access change requires a new baseline, identify that limitation in the care plan. Changing equipment for convenience alone does not establish medical necessity for an early repeat.

If an earlier report cannot be obtained, accept the attributable information described in section 20 when it establishes the indication and timing. Request the report itself only if an unresolved result, site, or technical detail is necessary to decide this request. Do not use lack of an attachment as proof that an earlier examination never occurred.

## 18. Related imaging and adjunctive measurements

Authorization of BMD under this policy does not automatically authorize vertebral fracture assessment (VFA), spine radiographs, MRI, nuclear scintigraphy, trabecular bone score analysis, or whole-body body-composition analysis. A combined order must identify each requested component and its clinical purpose.

VFA concerns vertebral fracture detection; it is not another name for BMD. [R2] If an adjunct is requested, assess its applicable benefit and medical-necessity provisions separately. Do not require VFA as a universal prerequisite for DXA, and do not infer that a reported vertebral fracture was confirmed by VFA unless the submitted evidence identifies that examination.

Suspected acute fracture, focal destructive disease, or malignancy may require a different diagnostic imaging pathway. Refer the requested additional service for its own assessment rather than declaring a bone-density request to be authorization for that workup.

Whole-body composition testing solely for fitness, weight management, or nonclinical wellness is outside this policy's BMD indications. An adjunct result is not mandatory evidence for any standard indication route in sections 4 through 14.

## 19. Alternative methods and specialized populations

DXA remains the standard method for this policy. Quantitative CT or another alternative BMD method requires individual physician review establishing an indication, why suitable DXA sites cannot answer the question, the proposed method's clinical role, and how its result will guide the named decision. A preference for a different technique alone is insufficient.

Do not authorize multiple methods to answer the same question without a separately documented purpose for each. Do not interpret an alternative method's number through a central-DXA threshold or compare it directly with a DXA result.

Heel ultrasound is not eligible as a substitute for DXA monitoring of osteoporosis pharmacotherapy. [R6] A peripheral finding may support a request for an appropriate central baseline under section 12.

Adults with spinal cord injury, substantial skeletal deformity, uncommon metabolic bone disease, or a need for specialized nonstandard sites require a clinician's site-specific assessment. Describe the population, the proposed sites, the decision to be informed, and the reason routine hip/spine measurements are inadequate. Do not apply a routine site protocol when it cannot address that clinical question.

Use a relevant indication route in sections 4 through 14 or a section 23 exception. A specialist's recommendation supports individual assessment but does not waive the need for a defined indication and interpretable measurement. Pediatric examinations remain outside the adult population in section 1 and require a population-appropriate review.

## 20. Core submission requirements and acceptable evidence sources

For every request, provide:

1. Member and ordering clinician identifiers, requested service date, and quantity.
2. The measurement method and proposed skeletal sites.
3. The request purpose: initial screening, diagnostic baseline, confirmatory baseline, treatment monitoring, untreated follow-up, or technical replacement.
4. A concise clinical account that establishes one applicable indication route.
5. Whether a prior BMD study exists and, for a repeat request, its date and the reason another measurement is needed.

Provide only the route-specific additions in section 21 that apply. Age-only screening does not require laboratory studies or an osteoporosis diagnosis. An initial diagnostic assessment does not require a previous DXA result. A valid report is not required to contain a recommendation for repeat testing if the treating clinician supplies the indication and timing rationale.

Accept signed clinical notes, relevant reports, medication records, and an attributable clinician-authored summary containing the facts necessary for the decision. If an original report is unavailable, identify the source and limitation of the summary; it may be sufficient when no material fact is disputed.

An AI-generated account is a draft and does not independently verify its underlying evidence. Preserve the distinction between an actual supplied report and a clinician narrative referring to that report. A narrative may support review without being represented as a separately attached or verified source document.

Do not require the same fact in multiple formats when a reliable submitted source already establishes it. Request a source record when a material inconsistency or uncertainty cannot be resolved from the available account. Unrelated imaging, complete lifetime records, and laboratory panels with no bearing on the selected route are not routine prerequisites.

## 21. Required evidence by selected indication

The following rows are **alternatives**, corresponding to the indication used. They are not cumulative requirements.

| Selected route | Minimum route-specific evidence | Information that is not universally required |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Age-based screening, section 4 | Age at requested date, applicable demographic context, screening order, and prior-study status | Osteoporosis diagnosis, fracture, FRAX, medications, or laboratory tests |
| Younger postmenopausal screening, section 5 | Menopausal status, qualifying risk factor, tool result and relevant inputs or documented validated assessment | A positive second tool or a prior abnormal scan |
| Men age 50-69, section 6 | Age, at least one listed factor, and stated assessment purpose | A positive FRAX result or multiple factors |
| Established osteoporosis, section 7.A | Clinician's diagnosis and basis, plus baseline or repeat purpose | A concurrent fracture or prior medication failure |
| Fragility fracture, section 7.B | Site, date/period, low-trauma mechanism or clinician's classification basis, and intended BMD use | An unhealed injury or active fracture episode |
| Suspicious imaging, section 7.C | Relevant finding, its source, and question to be resolved | Another imaging study if the submitted finding is already clear |
| Secondary cause, section 8 | Condition, relevant clinical activity, and management decision | Every endocrine, nutritional, or inflammatory laboratory result |
| Renal/transplant risk, section 9 | Renal stage/transplant context, skeletal-risk evidence, and decision affected | Bone biopsy or a complete renal workup for every request |
| Systemic steroids, section 10 | Agent, route, equivalent dose, start and anticipated duration, and assessment purpose | Waiting three months to complete a planned qualifying exposure |
| Other drug exposure, section 11 | Agent/regimen, timing, relevant skeletal risk, and assessment purpose | Established osteoporosis before a qualifying baseline |
| Treatment monitoring, section 13 | Therapy and start/change date, last study information, and intended decision | A new fracture, failure of two therapies, or deterioration |
| Untreated follow-up, section 14 | Prior result if relevant to the decision, last study date, and reassessment purpose | Pharmacotherapy solely to obtain a follow-up study |
| Early repeat or technical replacement, sections 16-17 | Prior date, specific early-testing circumstance or defect, corrective plan where relevant, and why now | Demonstrated bone loss when replacing an invalid examination |

If one route is fully supported, an unanswered question belonging solely to another route does not create a gap. Identify any remaining method, interval, or evidence issue separately from the supported indication.

## 22. Interpretation of submitted results and conflicting evidence

Use the population-appropriate report. In postmenopausal women and men age 50 or older, a valid diagnostic-site **T-score of -2.5 or lower** supports densitometric osteoporosis. For younger men and premenopausal women, **Z-scores** are generally the appropriate comparison; **-2.0 or lower** denotes density below that expected for age and is not, by itself, a universal diagnosis of osteoporosis. [R2]

For the T-score population above, classify an available valid result as follows: [R13]

| T-score | Classification |
| --- | --- |
| **-1.0 or higher** | Normal density |
| **Below -1.0 and above -2.5** | Low bone mass |
| **-2.5 or lower** | Densitometric osteoporosis |

Classification describes the measurement; it does not by itself determine this request's indication, repeat interval, or overall fracture risk.

These are interpretation rules for available evidence, not prerequisites for every BMD request. Neither a prior score of -2.5 nor a pathological fracture is required for a supported screening, medication-exposure, or secondary-cause route.

For a result used in assessment, identify its acquisition date, method, site, score type, value, and units where applicable. T-scores and Z-scores are dimensionless and are not percentages. Preserve measured BMD units and do not silently convert a volumetric CT measurement into an areal DXA value.

A change in T-score alone does not establish a meaningful measured density change. When the decision depends on progression or response, obtain comparable BMD values and the interpreting clinician's assessment of measurement variability. Do not report significant loss from an uncalibrated comparison. [R2]

Do not infer that a normal measurement at one site disproves a documented diagnosis at another valid site. Identify conflicting dates, values, methods, or clinical accounts and obtain the narrow clarification that affects this request. A missing report and a normal report are different findings.

Risk calculators require their actual inputs and appropriate population context. FRAX is not a treatment-response measure, and its age limitations must be respected. [R10] Do not create a calculated result from guessed inputs or treat a treatment-intervention percentage as this policy's universal DXA screening threshold.

## 23. Individual physician judgment and bounded exceptions

An exception may be considered for a clinically supported adult request that falls outside a standard indication or timing rule. Require **all** of the following:

1. The precise standard provision that is not met or does not adequately address the member's circumstances.
2. The actual clinical findings, exposure, or technical limitation supporting the exception.
3. A named decision that the measurement will inform.
4. Why the proposed method, sites, and timing can answer that question.
5. Why an existing result, standard eligible interval, or other applicable route does not adequately address the question.

Examples suitable for consideration include a clinically important risk not captured by a screening tool, a substantial medication exposure outside the standard daily-dose pattern, an uncommon secondary cause, and a specialized site requirement. The presence of one example is not itself approval.

For a request under 12 months, section 16 additionally controls the timing assessment. For an alternative method, section 19 additionally controls method selection. These requirements cannot be bypassed by labeling a request an exception.

The ordering clinician's rationale is considered on its merits. A specialist consultation is requested only when the unresolved clinical issue requires that expertise; it is not mandatory for every exception. Statements such as "physician discretion," "high risk," or "medically necessary" without the supporting facts are insufficient.

A designated physician reviewer must document approval or nonapproval of the exception, the evidence relied on, the provision addressed, and the scope of the determination. Approval is case specific and does not modify the policy or establish a recurring authorization. Exceptions cannot create eligibility in another product or override a documented benefit exclusion.

## 24. Requests without established medical necessity

After the applicable evidence has been reviewed and any material clarification obtained, medical necessity is not established in the following circumstances:

- Screening below the specified age without a supported risk, diagnostic, secondary-cause, medication, or exception route.
- A requested repeat before the eligible interval without the required rationale or an approved exception.
- Duplication of an adequate recent study solely because the provider, referral, or preferred facility changed.
- Multiple methods or additional sites addressing the same question without a distinct documented clinical purpose.
- A monitoring request whose stated question cannot be answered by the proposed technique or timing.
- Testing solely for nonclinical curiosity, fitness, or wellness without an eligible clinical indication.

A generic diagnosis code or a bare request to "rule out osteoporosis" does not establish a route when the needed population, risk, clinical, or timing facts are absent. Classify that record as **insufficient evidence** until the necessary facts are clarified; do not assume the member lacks the condition.

Historical fracture, normal prior BMD, absence of pharmacotherapy, or age below a screening threshold is not a stand-alone reason to reject a different supported route. Review the alternatives before documenting a clinical nonapproval.

Separate three outcomes: service outside this document's scope, benefit not established, and clinical necessity not established. Each needs its own reason and applicable authority. An incomplete submission is not equivalent to a confirmed nonqualifying request.

## 25. Reviewer assessment and targeted requests for information

Review in this order:

1. Confirm product, population, service date, requested method and sites, and the document version applicable to the case.
2. Identify the indication route supported by the supplied facts. Evaluate alternative routes when needed; do not demand every route.
3. Establish initial versus repeat purpose and apply the timing rules.
4. Identify an exception, technical limitation, or conflict requiring physician assessment.
5. Record the supported findings, actual unresolved gaps, and resulting next action.

If evidence is incomplete, ask for the smallest item that resolves the gap. For example, request the steroid dose and expected duration for section 10, the last study date for section 15, or the invalid-site explanation for section 17. Cite the affected subsection and explain how its answer changes the assessment.

Do not request a fracture history solely to complete the osteoporosis route when it is not required. Do not ask again for a treatment purpose or a prior result already stated adequately in the submitted account. A preferred additional source may strengthen confidence, but it is not a mandatory gap unless a provision or material conflict requires it.

A review may conclude that clinical information is sufficient and recommend the ordinary human determination without a request for more evidence. If no material gap is identified, say so consistently; do not simultaneously recommend clarification of an already answered question.

When physician review is needed, state the clinical question being referred. Record whether the question concerns an exception, timing, modality, or interpretation. Administrative workflow status is documented separately from this medical assessment.

## 26. Authorization scope, quantity, and subsequent requests

A standard authorization concerns **one BMD acquisition episode** using the approved method and sites. The customary spine and hip components of that episode are not two independently approved repeat procedures. Additional independently acquired episodes require their own clinical purpose and timing assessment.

Record the actual authorized service, sites, quantity, and validity dates in the determination. This policy does not prescribe a universal authorization-expiration period or reimbursement amount; apply the product's administrative provisions and state the dates chosen for the case. A monitoring plan in the clinical note is not blanket authorization for all future scans.

If the date, method, or sites change materially before acquisition, assess whether the existing authorization remains applicable. A date change can affect both eligibility and the interval boundary. A minor administrative rescheduling does not automatically require a new clinical workup, but it cannot make an earlier ineligible interval appear eligible.

An approved DXA does not authorize osteoporosis medication, a procedure at another site for a different clinical purpose, or ancillary imaging listed in section 18. The submitted billing claim must identify the service actually performed.

For another BMD request, use the actual last usable acquisition date, not the date of the previous authorization or the date its result was transcribed into a note.

## 28. Decision record, assisted drafting, and reconsideration

Record the case identifier, policy number and version, requested date, selected route and relevant subsection, evidence sources, timing calculation where applicable, and any exception rationale. The determination must distinguish a supported indication from an unresolved method or timing question.

A clinical approval records the authorized scope. A clinical nonapproval identifies the actual provision not met and the facts supporting that finding. A request for more information identifies the missing fact, why it is material, and the evidence already accepted. Do not use a generic list of all policy documents as the case-specific explanation.

Assisted summaries and proposed rationales are drafts. The reviewer verifies their clinical facts, section references, and findings before use. An assistant must not invent a report, infer an unsubmitted risk score, turn a review step into a patient criterion, or submit an authorization decision. The authorized reviewer makes and records the final determination.

For reconsideration, retain the previous rationale and identify the new or corrected evidence, policy provision, and resulting change or unchanged finding. Apply the product's applicable notice and appeal provisions; this clinical document does not create a statutory deadline or limit a member's rights.

Do not describe an absent source as verified or an unresolved question as answered. Preserve enough traceability to explain which clinical facts and policy provisions supported the case-specific result.
